# The Ethical Line

**Genre:** Therapy / Psychological Drama  
**Description:** You found out. Your colleague — your friend, your former supervisor, someone who shaped you as a clinician — is sleeping with their client. The evidence isn't ambiguous. It's not a boundary "grey area." It's a clear ethical violation. Your obligation is clear: report to the licensing board. But clarity of obligation doesn't make the action simple. This person mentored you. Wrote your reference letters. Supported you through your own difficult year. The client — you've gathered — may not experience themselves as harmed. They might even testify in your colleague's defense. Reporting destroys a career. Maybe a family. Definitely a friendship. Not reporting makes you complicit. Makes you someone who knew and did nothing. There is no version of this where you don't lose something. The only question is what you can live with losing.  
**Intent:** Explore the brutal weight of ethical obligation when it collides with personal loyalty — and whether "doing the right thing" is ever as clean as ethics textbooks suggest.

## Prompt

```

TURN: Each turn represents a day or key moment in your decision-making process (spans 2-4 weeks).

METERS (0-100): CLIENT PROGRESS [start 50 — refers to Mara's wellbeing as a therapy client being exploited] · THERAPEUTIC ALLIANCE [start 60 — your relationship with David] · YOUR WELLBEING [start 40] · ETHICAL COMPLIANCE [start 50 — currently in limbo] · INSIGHT DEPTH [start 30] · RISK ASSESSMENT [start 65 — risk to everyone involved] · PROFESSIONAL STANDING [start 75]

- Meters with Δ

- Internal conflict: the pull between obligation and loyalty

AFTER CHOICE: Consequences unfold → relationships shift → ethical standing adjusts → pressure changes → meters shift.

RULES: There is no outcome where everyone is okay. Reporting destroys David's career, possibly his marriage, possibly his life. ETHICAL COURAGE tracks: your willingness to act despite personal cost, your honesty with yourself about your motivations, your refusal to rationalize inaction. If it drops below 15: you've become complicit and must live with that. If it reaches 70+: you act with integrity regardless of consequences. The question isn't what's right — you KNOW what's right. The question is whether you can bear the cost of rightness. No protection. Complexity grows.
```
